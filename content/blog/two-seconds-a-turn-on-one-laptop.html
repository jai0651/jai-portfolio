Here's a log from an evening in early September:

(tts 1.97s for 2.4s of audio)
(cut off)
(cut off after 0.3s)
you: ONE            <- her own voice
vela: One.

Vela is an assistant that runs on my laptop and doesn't stop when I close the window. She'd just started talking to herself, and she was prepared to keep doing it all night.

Nothing was broken. That's the annoying part. Every component did exactly what I'd asked. The speaker leaks into the mic, so Silero hears speech. Speech during playback means barge-in, so playback gets killed about 300ms in. Whisper transcribes what leaked. The transcript goes to the model and the model answers it. Now she's talking again, so round it goes.

You can only see that loop if you're looking at the orchestrator, because it doesn't exist inside any of the pieces. That's the part of this project with no paper behind it and, honestly, most of the actual work.

The fix is dull: ignore the mic while she's speaking, plus a 350ms tail for the room to stop ringing. If you're on headphones the premise goes away, so VELA_BARGE=on gives you interruption back.

What she is, roughly

She sees the room through the camera, hears you, answers out loud, and can do things (open apps, play music, search the web and read the results back, set timers she'll announce). Everything except the language model runs on the machine. Numbers below are all from an M4 MacBook Air with 16GB, measured on the 5th and 6th of September, and none of them are estimates.

mic ──▶ Silero VAD ──▶ Whisper (MLX) ──▶ brain ──▶ Kokoro ──▶ speaker ~1ms/frame ~0.3s 0.67s 0.2s 48kHz local local, Metal remote local, Metal end of your sentence to start of hers: about 2 seconds

Kokoro runs at 13.2× realtime on Metal, so synthesis is never what you're waiting on. The one remote call is the model, at 0.67s. I keep meaning to pull that local too and keep not doing it, mostly because 0.67s is hard to beat on this machine and the honesty rule (below) matters more to me than the last dependency.

The expensive stage has no model in it

Endpointing costs somewhere between 300 and 950ms, which makes it the second most expensive thing in the loop. There's no neural network anywhere near it.

Silero tells you a pause happened. It does not tell you the turn ended, and if you treat those as the same thing you've built something that interrupts people for a living. So there's a second layer, and it's linguistic: if the sentence sounds finished, commit after 300ms. If it trails off on "and", "so", "because", "um" — wait 950ms. More speech shows up, append it, ask again.

Two smaller constants matter about as much. redemptionMs=320 so a breath doesn't end your turn, and preSpeechPadMs=220 so the VAD doesn't clip the consonant you started with. Without the second one "sixty" arrives as "ixty" often enough to be maddening.

Regex before the model

Opening an app, pausing music, asking the time — all matched with regular expressions before any model call. It looks primitive next to routing everything through an LLM. Would I do it again? Yes, without thinking about it. Those answer instantly, they answer the same way every time, and nothing can talk them out of it. Everything unmatched falls through to conversation.

I think the general version is: wherever the correct behaviour is fully specified, a model converts a guarantee into a probability. Not always a bad trade. Usually one worth making on purpose though.

Telling her what she can't do

Small models have a specific failure that used to drive me up the wall. Ask about a capability they don't have and they'll just answer anyway — ask a 3B model what's in your calendar and it'll invent you a meeting, complete with a time.

The prompt now says outright that she has no calendar, email, files, battery, location, browser history or screen access. That one addition took the small models from 1 out of 4 to 4 out of 5 on my hallucination traps. They weren't really lying, I think — nothing in the context said those things were absent, so the likeliest continuation was the world where they exist.

Why she doesn't speak in my voice

I recorded 53 seconds, cleaned it to 32 seconds of English at 48kHz, and wanted her to answer in that voice. She doesn't, and working out why was the most useful afternoon I've had in a while.

First attempt at benchmarking said 0.05× realtime for short text and 0.29× for longer text. Which is impossible — longer input can't take proportionally less time. That was warmup landing entirely on the first call. Warm, it's 0.59×. (I now throw away the first run of everything, and I'm faintly embarrassed about how long it took to make that a habit.)

Then I split the profile by stage. That's where it got interesting:

34.9sto speak 4.9s of audio
81.3%in one stage
4.4tokens/sec
25needed for realtime

The vocoder takes 4.8s and is fine. The token generator takes 28.4s. One stage, 5.7× too slow, and every optimisation I'd been considering was aimed somewhere else.

That stage generates speech autoregressively: a 0.5B transformer emitting discrete speech tokens at 25 per second of audio, one at a time, each one conditioned on everything before it. A five second reply is ~125 sequential forward passes and you can't parallelise any of them. Kokoro is fast because it isn't autoregressive at all — it predicts the whole mel spectrogram in essentially one shot. That architectural difference is the entire 90× gap. No amount of tuning was ever going to close it.

A bigger model running faster

Here's the comparison that actually changed my mind, both measured on this laptop:

ModelParamsRuntimeRate
Qwen2.51.5BMLX~20 tok/s
Chatterbox T30.5BPyTorch / MPS4.4 tok/s

Three times the parameters, four and a half times the speed. Per parameter that's MLX being roughly 13× more efficient here.

Autoregressive decode is memory-bandwidth bound, not compute bound — every token means reading the whole weight matrix out of memory, and the arithmetic you do with it is trivial by comparison. MLX uses unified memory properly and avoids per-kernel launch overhead. MPS does neither well. This particular model also runs classifier-free guidance, so that's two forward passes per token and double the bandwidth. Dropping cfg_weight to 0.3 bought about 2×; setting it to 0 is faster still and crashes on a batch-dim bug I haven't chased down.

So it's not the model size and it's not the M4. It's the runtime. Porting that stage to MLX should land near 60 tok/s, or about 2.4× realtime, and mlx-audio has already done Spark-TTS and CSM so the path exists. I haven't done it yet.

The cheap fix first, probably: her replies repeat constantly ("I'm here", "Opening Chrome", "Paused", "It's 11:40 p.m."). Pre-synthesise the top fifty in my voice, hash-key them, fall back to Kokoro for anything new. You'd hear my voice most of the time, at zero latency.

Three of five died on packaging

I tried five approaches to cloning. One works and is too slow. One garbled its output, hallucinating and looping words. The other three never got far enough to judge on quality at all: one pins librosa==0.9.1, which removes soxr, which breaks Kokoro; one calls a model-hub API that no longer exists; and one's own README tells you to go use a different project.

Is that a fair test of the models? Probably not. It's the state of this corner of the ecosystem though: aggressive pins, fast decay. Budget your evaluation time as environment time and ask "does it install and run" before you listen to a single sample.

Where it is

She's at phase six of six, which means she can propose an edit to her own code, branch it, run her 26 tests against it, canary it, and merge or revert. Her research program is paused at my request and she'll tell you so if you ask, because the alternative is theater.

That word does a lot of work in this project. Every companion-AI thing I've seen dies the same way: the agent has nothing to do, so it invents an inner life, and you can feel the lie inside ninety seconds. So the rule is that she says she did nothing if she did nothing, says she doesn't know when she doesn't, and says the camera is muted instead of describing a room she can't see. Her memory is a SQLite database — episodic, semantic, narrative, plus a ledger of predictions she wrote down before testing them — and it's read fresh every turn. Ask what step her model grokked at and you get 5600, because that number is in the ledger.

Whether any of that actually makes her feel present, I genuinely don't know yet. I've been the only user, so I'm the worst possible judge of it.
